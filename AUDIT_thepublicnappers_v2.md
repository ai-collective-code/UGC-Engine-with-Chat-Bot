## Scope and honesty statement

This audit covers **11 followers out of 1,162**. Every number below was read directly from the live profile pages on 6 August 2026 — none are estimated, modelled, or scored.

**These 11 are not a random sample.** They came from a supplied list with no stated selection method. The findings below describe *these 11 accounts* and the network they belong to. They do **not** establish a bot percentage for the full 1,162, and this report does not extrapolate one. See *Limits* at the end.

A prior version of this audit (2 files, dated 5–6 Aug) contained invented figures — a "45/100 health score", a "~625 bot followers" extrapolation, and fabricated confidence percentages. Those files are superseded and should be discarded.

## 3. The central finding: a coordinated network

This is the part that matters more than the headline percentage.

Following counts across the eight flagged accounts:

```
2,205 · 2,918 · 4,217 · 4,431 · 4,636 · 4,920 · 6,860
```

Seven of eight cluster in a narrow **2,000–7,000** band. Random spam does not distribute this way. This is the signature of **a single follow-farm network or one purchased follower package** — the accounts were provisioned together and operate on the same follow budget.

**Corroborating evidence — the network is live right now.** The supplied CSV recorded `duck.49181321` at 9 followers / 4,650 following. On 6 August it reads **13 / 4,636**. It has gained followers and dropped follows since the export. These are not dormant shells; they are actively churning.

**Practical implication:** removing these one at a time is futile while the source is still feeding. The acquisition channel has to be identified and stopped first.

## 4. What could not be measured

Stated plainly, because a report that hides its gaps is worse than useless:

| Not measured | Why | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| **Engagement rate** | Likes and comments are not visible logged out | Instagram Insights (account owner) |
| Reach, impressions, saves | Owner-only metrics | Instagram Insights |
| Follower demographics | Owner-only | Instagram Insights → Audience |
| Post counts per follower | Not rendered logged out | Logged-in inspection |
| Account creation dates | Not exposed by Instagram | Unavailable |
| The other 1,151 followers | Not supplied | See recommendation 1 |

**Engagement rate is the single most valuable missing number.** If the bot ratio here is anywhere near representative, real engagement will be running at roughly a third of what the follower count implies. That gap is the actual business cost, and it cannot be quantified without Insights access.

## 5. Recommendations

Ordered by what the evidence actually supports.

### First — establish the real number

Nothing else should be decided on an 11-account sample. Pull **100–150 randomly selected followers** and run the same live check. That yields a bot percentage defensible to a client or to Springfit. Everything below is provisional until this exists.

### Second — find and close the source

The clustered following counts point to one origin. Determine whether it was:
- a paid follower package,
- a follow-for-follow / engagement pod, or
- an agency or contractor buying growth on the brand's behalf.

Cleanup before the source is closed will simply be refilled.

### Third — get Insights access

Convert to (or confirm) a Business/Creator account and open Insights. This unlocks engagement rate, reach and demographics — and makes every future audit far stronger than what a logged-out inspection can produce.

### Fourth — then remove, in order of certainty

Start with the seven definitive cases (ratio over 100): `anish0099912`, `hawk.7181163`, `x__akash_._s_`, `duck.49181321`, `sad_mundapb03`, `tahmina__laskar__98`, `prisca_miss02`. Instagram's *Remove Follower* is preferable to blocking — quieter, and reversible.

Hold `itz.bad_boy_7310` (ratio 13.8) for manual review. It is the weakest case in the set and could be a real low-activity user.

### What not to change

The content operation is working — ~5 posts per week, a sound reels/carousel mix, clear brand positioning and a live link to springfit.com. **The problem is the audience, not the content.** Do not let a follower-quality finding trigger a content overhaul.

---

## 6. Limits of this report

1. **11 of 1,162 followers examined — 0.9% coverage.** No population-level claim is made or supported.
2. **The sample was not randomly drawn.** Selection method unknown; it may be biased toward or against bots.
3. **Ratio-based classification is strong but not infallible.** A real user who follows thousands can be caught by it; `kumar__king_1s` sat in the gray zone and was resolved on qualitative signals.
4. **All figures are a 6 August 2026 snapshot.** `duck.49181321` demonstrably moved between the CSV export and this audit.
5. **No engagement data.** The commercial impact of the bot followers is unquantified.

---

**Bottom line:** a coordinated bot network is present in this account's follower base, it is still active, and the affected accounts trace to a common source. The content strategy is sound. The immediate priorities are a properly sized sample, identification of the acquisition channel, and Insights access — in that order.
